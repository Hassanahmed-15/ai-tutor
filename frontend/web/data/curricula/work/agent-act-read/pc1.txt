# Unit 1: Polynomial and Rational Functions
## 1.1 Change in Tandem
@ Functions and how outputs vary
1.1.A|Describe how a function's inputs and outputs vary together by comparing function values.
1.1.A.1|Define function, domain, range, independent and dependent variables, and when two functions are equal.
1.1.A.2|Know a function rule can be graphical, numerical, analytic or verbal; understand image and preimage.
1.1.A.3|Recognize a function as increasing on an interval when larger inputs always give larger outputs.
1.1.A.4|Recognize a function as decreasing on an interval when larger inputs always give smaller outputs.
@ Graphs of changing quantities
1.1.B|Build a graph of two quantities that vary together in a real situation.
1.1.B.1|Know a graph shows input-output pairs and how the values vary together.
1.1.B.2|Use a verbal description of how things change together to build a graph.
1.1.B.3|Identify concave up where the rate of change is increasing.
1.1.B.4|Identify concave down where the rate of change is decreasing.
1.1.B.5|Know the graph meets the x-axis at zeros, where output is zero.
## 1.2 Rates of Change
@ Average rate of change
1.2.A|Compare rates of change at two points using average rates of change near them.
1.2.A.1|Define the average rate of change over an interval as output change divided by input change.
1.2.A.2|Approximate the rate of change at a point using average rates over small intervals around it.
1.2.A.3|Compare rates of change at two points using small-interval average rates.
@ Meaning of rate of change
1.2.B|Describe how two quantities vary together at different points and over different intervals.
1.2.B.1|Know rates of change measure how two quantities vary together.
1.2.B.2|Read a positive rate of change as both quantities moving in the same direction.
1.2.B.3|Read a negative rate of change as one quantity rising while the other falls.
## 1.3 Rates of Change in Linear and Quadratic Functions
@ Average rates for linear and quadratic functions
1.3.A|Find average rates of change for sequences and functions, including linear and quadratic.
1.3.A.1|Know a linear function has a constant average rate of change.
1.3.A.2|Know a quadratic function's average rates over equal-length intervals form a linear function.
1.3.A.3|Interpret the average rate of change over [a, b] as the slope of a secant line.
@ Change in the rates of change
1.3.B|Find how average rates of change change for linear, quadratic and other functions.
1.3.B.1|Know a linear function's average rates change at a rate of zero.
1.3.B.2|Know a quadratic function's average rates change at a constant rate.
1.3.B.3|Link increasing average rates to concave up and decreasing average rates to concave down.
## 1.4 Polynomial Functions and Rates of Change
@ Polynomial features and extrema
1.4.A|Identify key features of polynomial functions related to rates of change.
1.4.A.1|Define a polynomial function, its degree, leading term and leading coefficient.
1.4.A.2|Identify local and global maxima and minima where a polynomial switches between increasing and decreasing.
1.4.A.3|Know a local extremum lies between every two distinct real zeros.
1.4.A.4|Know even-degree polynomials have a global max or min, found at the vertex for quadratics.
1.4.A.5|Locate points of inflection where concavity changes.
## 1.5 Polynomial Functions and Complex Zeros
@ Zeros and multiplicity
1.5.A|Identify key features of polynomials related to zeros when factorizations or technology are available.
1.5.A.1|Define zeros, roots and linear factors, and link real zeros to linear factors.
1.5.A.2|Define multiplicity; a degree-n polynomial has n complex zeros counting multiplicity.
1.5.A.3|Link real zeros to x-intercepts and to endpoints of polynomial inequality intervals.
1.5.A.4|Know non-real zeros come in conjugate pairs.
1.5.A.5|Know even-multiplicity zeros keep the output sign and make the graph tangent to the x-axis.
1.5.A.6|Find the degree of a polynomial from constant successive differences of outputs.
@ Even and odd polynomials
1.5.B|Decide whether a polynomial function is even or odd.
1.5.B.1|Know even functions are symmetric over x = 0 with f(-x) = f(x).
1.5.B.2|Know odd functions are symmetric about the origin with f(-x) = -f(x).

## 1.7 Rational Functions and End Behavior
@ End behavior of rational functions
1.7.A|Describe end behavior of rational functions.
1.7.A.1|Define a rational function as a quotient of polynomials comparing numerator to denominator size.
1.7.A.2|Find end behavior from the quotient of leading terms.
1.7.A.3|Find a polynomial end behavior or slant asymptote when the numerator dominates.
1.7.A.4|Find a horizontal asymptote at a constant when neither polynomial dominates.
1.7.A.5|Find a horizontal asymptote at y = 0 when the denominator dominates.
1.7.A.6|Use limit notation for horizontal asymptotes.
## 1.8 Rational Functions and Zeros
@ Zeros of rational functions

1.8.A.1|Know real zeros of a rational function come from real zeros of its numerator within the domain.
1.8.A.2|Use zeros of numerator and denominator as endpoints or asymptotes for rational inequality intervals.
## 1.9 Rational Functions and Vertical Asymptotes
@ Vertical asymptotes
1.9.A|Find vertical asymptotes of graphs of rational functions.
1.9.A.1|Locate vertical asymptotes at denominator zeros that are not cancelled by the numerator.

## 1.10 Rational Functions and Holes
@ Holes in graphs
1.10.A|Find holes in graphs of rational functions.
1.10.A.1|Know a hole occurs where the numerator's zero multiplicity is at least the denominator's.
1.10.A.2|Find a hole's location from outputs near the input, using limit notation.

1.11.A.3|Use information from different forms of the same function to answer contextual questions.
1.11.B|Divide polynomial functions using long division.
1.11.B.1|Rewrite f as g times quotient plus remainder, with remainder degree below the divisor.

@ Binomial theorem
1.11.C|Rewrite repeated products of binomials using the binomial theorem.
1.11.C.1|Expand (a + b)^n using a row of Pascal's Triangle, including (x + c)^n.
## 1.12 Transformations of Functions
@ Translations and dilations
1.12.A|Build a function that is an additive or multiplicative transformation of another function.
1.12.A.1|Translate vertically with g(x) = f(x) + k.
1.12.A.2|Translate horizontally with g(x) = f(x + h), a shift by -h.
1.12.A.3|Dilate vertically with g(x) = a f(x), reflecting over the x-axis if a is negative.
1.12.A.4|Dilate horizontally with g(x) = f(bx), reflecting over the y-axis if b is negative.
1.12.A.5|Combine transformations; distinguish preimage from image.
1.12.A.6|Know a transformed function can have a different domain and range from its parent.
