# Unit 3: Trigonometric and Polar Functions
## 3.1 Periodic Phenomena
@ Periodic relationships
3.1.A|Build graphs of periodic relationships from verbal descriptions.
3.1.A.1|Recognize a periodic relationship as outputs repeating over successive equal-length input intervals.
3.1.A.2|Build a periodic graph from one cycle.
3.1.B|Describe key features of a periodic function from a verbal description.
3.1.B.1|Define the period as the smallest positive k with f(x + k) = f(x).
3.1.B.2|Estimate the period by finding where the output pattern repeats.
3.1.B.3|Know every characteristic in one period appears in every period.
## 3.2 Sine, Cosine, and Tangent
@ Angles and the unit circle
3.2.A|Find sine, cosine and tangent of an angle using the unit circle.
3.2.A.1|Place angles in standard position; use positive and negative rotation and coterminal angles.
3.2.A.2|Define radian measure as arc length divided by radius.
3.2.A.3|Define sine as the vertical displacement ratio, the y-coordinate on the unit circle.
3.2.A.4|Define cosine as the horizontal displacement ratio, the x-coordinate on the unit circle.
3.2.A.5|Define tangent as the slope of the terminal ray, sine over cosine.

@ Graphing sine and cosine
3.4.A|Build representations of sine and cosine using the unit circle.
3.4.A.1|Define sin t as the y-coordinate of the unit circle point, with domain all reals.
3.4.A.2|See sine oscillate between -1 and 1 as the angle grows.
3.4.A.3|Define cos t as the x-coordinate of the unit circle point, with domain all reals.

## 3.5 Sinusoidal Functions
@ Features of sinusoidal functions
3.5.A|Identify key features of sine and cosine.
3.5.A.1|Define sinusoidal functions as transformations of sine; cosine is sine shifted by pi/2.
3.5.A.2|Treat period and frequency as reciprocals; the period of sine and cosine is 2 pi.
3.5.A.3|Define amplitude as half the max-min difference.
3.5.A.4|Find the midline as the average of max and min.
3.5.A.5|Know sinusoids alternate between concave down and concave up.
3.5.A.6|Know sine is odd and cosine is even.
## 3.6 Sinusoidal Function Transformations
@ Transforming sinusoids
3.6.A|Identify amplitude, vertical shift, period and phase shift of a sinusoid.
3.6.A.1|Recognize the form a sin(b(t + c)) + d, with the same transformations for sine and cosine.
3.6.A.2|Treat adding d as a vertical translation that moves the midline.
3.6.A.3|Treat adding c inside as a phase shift of -c units.
3.6.A.4|Treat the factor a as a vertical dilation that changes amplitude.
3.6.A.5|Treat the factor b as a horizontal dilation that changes period.
3.6.A.6|Read amplitude |a|, period 2 pi/|b|, vertical shift d and phase shift -c from the form.
## 3.7 Sinusoidal Function Context and Data Modeling
@ Modeling with sinusoids
3.7.A|Build sinusoidal models of periodic phenomena.
3.7.A.1|Estimate period and frequency from consecutive maxima or minima.
3.7.A.2|Find amplitude and vertical shift from the maximum and minimum.
3.7.A.3|Estimate phase shift by comparing an actual data pair to the model.
3.7.A.4|Build models with technology by estimating key values or using sinusoidal regression.
3.7.A.5|Use sinusoidal models within their contextual domain to predict values and solve for inputs.
## 3.8 The Tangent Function
@ Tangent function and its graph
3.8.A|Build representations of tangent using the unit circle.
3.8.A.1|Define tan t as the slope of the terminal ray.
3.8.A.2|Write tan t = sin t / cos t where cosine is nonzero.
3.8.B|Describe key features of tangent.
3.8.B.1|Know tangent has period pi.
3.8.B.2|Locate vertical asymptotes at pi/2 + k pi.
3.8.B.3|Know tangent increases and switches from concave down to concave up between asymptotes.
@ Transforming tangent
3.8.C|Describe additive and multiplicative transformations of tangent.
3.8.C.1|Treat adding d as a vertical translation of the graph and its inflection line.
3.8.C.2|Treat adding c inside as a phase shift of -c units.
3.8.C.3|Treat a as a vertical dilation, reflecting over the x-axis if negative.
3.8.C.4|Treat b as a horizontal dilation that changes the period, reflecting over the y-axis if negative.
3.8.C.5|Read the form a tan(b(t + c)) + d: dilation a, period pi/|b|, shift d, phase shift -c.

## 3.11 The Secant, Cosecant, and Cotangent Functions
@ Reciprocal trig functions

3.11.A.1|Define secant as the reciprocal of cosine.
