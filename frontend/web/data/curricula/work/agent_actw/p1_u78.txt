T|7.1 Defining Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)
S|What simple harmonic motion is
7.1.A|Describe simple harmonic motion
7.1.A.1|SHM is a special case of periodic motion
7.1.A.2|SHM occurs when the restoring force is proportional to displacement from equilibrium; small-angle pendulums qualify
T|7.2 Frequency and Period of SHM
S|Frequency and period
7.2.A|Describe the frequency and period of an object in SHM
7.2.A.1|Period is the inverse of frequency; know the period formulas for a spring oscillator and a small-angle pendulum
T|7.3 Representing and Analyzing SHM
S|Displacement, velocity and acceleration in SHM
7.3.A|Describe displacement, velocity and acceleration of an object in SHM
7.3.A.1|Displacement follows a cosine or sine of time; know where velocity and acceleration peak or vanish
7.3.A.2|Changing amplitude does not change period
7.3.A.3|Analyse SHM properties from graphs
T|7.4 Energy of Simple Harmonic Oscillators
S|Energy in SHM
7.4.A|Describe the mechanical energy of a system in SHM
7.4.A.1|Total energy is kinetic plus potential
7.4.A.2|Total energy in SHM is constant
7.4.A.3|Kinetic energy is greatest where potential energy is least
7.4.A.4|Potential energy is greatest where kinetic energy is zero; amplitude sets the total energy
U|Unit 8: Fluids
T|8.1 Internal Structure and Density
S|Fluid properties and density
8.1.A|Describe the properties of a fluid
8.1.A.1|Solids, liquids and gases differ by how their atoms and molecules interact
8.1.A.2|A fluid has no fixed shape
8.1.A.3|Density is mass per unit volume
8.1.A.4|An ideal fluid is incompressible and has no viscosity
T|8.2 Pressure
S|Pressure on a surface
8.2.A|Describe the pressure from a force on a surface
8.2.A.1|Pressure is perpendicular force per unit area
8.2.A.2|Pressure is a scalar
8.2.A.3|An incompressible fluid keeps constant volume and density under pressure
S|Fluid pressure
8.2.B|Describe the pressure exerted by a fluid
8.2.B.1|Fluid pressure comes from particle interactions with the surface
8.2.B.2|Absolute pressure is reference (such as atmospheric) plus gauge pressure
8.2.B.3|Gauge pressure of a vertical fluid column depends on density, g and depth
T|8.3 Fluids and Newton's Laws
S|Fluid motion
8.3.A|Describe when a fluid's velocity changes
8.3.A.1|Newton's laws describe motion of particles in a fluid
8.3.A.2|Fluid behaviour results from internal interactions and external forces
S|Buoyant force
8.3.B|Describe the buoyant force on an object in a fluid
8.3.B.1|Buoyant force is a net upward force from the fluid
8.3.B.2|It results from the collective forces of fluid particles on the object
8.3.B.3|Its size equals the weight of displaced fluid
T|8.4 Fluids and Conservation Laws
S|Continuity of flow
8.4.A|Describe incompressible flow through a cross-section using mass conservation
8.4.A.1|A pressure difference drives flow; flow rate depends on cross-sectional area and speed
8.4.A.2|The continuity equation says area times speed is constant
S|Bernoulli and Torricelli
8.4.B|Describe flow resulting from an energy difference in the fluid-Earth system
8.4.B.1|Height differences produce differences in kinetic energy and pressure by conservation laws
8.4.B.2|Bernoulli's equation expresses conservation of mechanical energy in flow
