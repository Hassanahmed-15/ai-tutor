T|3.6 p-Values
S|Interpreting a p-value
3.6.A|Interpret the p-value of a test for a proportion
3.6.A.1|The p-value is the probability, assuming the null is true, of a test statistic as extreme as observed, in the direction of Ha
3.6.A.2|With a simulated null distribution, the p-value is the proportion of simulated values as extreme
3.6.A.3|State that the p-value assumes the null hypothesis is true
3.6.A.4|Small p-values give evidence for the alternative
3.6.A.5|Large p-values are not evidence for the null
T|3.7 Carrying Out a Test for a Population Proportion
S|Test statistic and p-value
3.7.A|Calculate a test statistic and p-value for a proportion
3.7.A.1|The z-statistic is (p-hat minus p0) over the standard error under the null
3.7.A.2|The null distribution can be approximated by a probability model such as the standard normal
3.7.A.3|Find the p-value from the standard normal using a table or technology
S|Decisions and conclusions
3.7.B|Justify a claim from the results of a test for a proportion
3.7.B.1|The significance level alpha is the preset probability of rejecting a true null
3.7.B.2|Reject the null if the p-value is below alpha; otherwise fail to reject
3.7.B.3|Rejecting means convincing evidence for the alternative; failing to reject means not convincing
3.7.B.4|A test never proves the null true
3.7.B.5|Test results support answers to the investigative question
3.7.B.6|State the conclusion in context, tied to the alternative, with non-definitive language
T|3.8 Potential Errors When Performing Tests
S|Type I and Type II errors
3.8.A|Identify Type I and Type II errors
3.8.A.1|Type I error: convincing evidence for Ha when Ha is false
3.8.A.2|Type II error: not convincing evidence for Ha when Ha is true
3.8.A.3|Power is the probability of correctly rejecting a false null
3.8.B|Calculate the probability of Type I and Type II errors
3.8.B.1|Type I error probability equals the significance level alpha
3.8.B.2|Type II error probability is 1 minus power
S|Factors and consequences
3.8.C|Identify factors affecting the probability of errors
3.8.C.1|Type II error falls and power rises with larger samples, smaller standard error, a parameter farther from the null, or larger alpha
3.8.D|Interpret Type I and Type II errors
3.8.D.1|Either error may be more serious depending on the study
3.8.D.2|Type I consequences influence the choice of alpha
3.8.D.3|Type II consequences influence the choice of sample size
