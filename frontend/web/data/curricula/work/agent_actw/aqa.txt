G|all
U|3.1 Summary of subject content
T|Titles and example areas of study
S|The six endorsed titles
3.1|Know the six titles and their example areas of study; example areas are neither compulsory nor exclusive
-|Art, craft and design: practical and critical/contextual work using 2D and/or 3D processes and media from two or more titles
-|Fine art: drawing, painting, mixed media, sculpture, ceramics, installation, printmaking, moving image and photography
-|Graphic communication: interactive media, advertising, packaging, print, illustration, branding, motion graphics and film and television design
-|Textile design: fashion, costume, digital and printed textiles, domestic textiles, interiors and installed textiles
-|Three-dimensional design: ceramics, sculpture, exhibition, theatre and film design, product, environmental and architectural design, jewellery and 3D digital design
-|Photography: portraiture, landscape, still life, documentary, fashion, experimental imagery, multimedia, installation and moving image
U|3.2 Overarching knowledge, understanding and skills
T|Knowledge and understanding
S|What students must understand
3.2|Experience a variety of traditional and new media, processes and techniques suited to the chosen areas, building knowledge through research, idea development and making
-|Take an active part in the course, recognising your strengths and sustaining your own lines of enquiry
-|Develop practical and theoretical knowledge of materials, processes, technologies and resources
-|Understand how ideas, feelings and meanings are conveyed and interpreted in images and artefacts
-|Understand how images and artefacts relate to the time, place and social and cultural contexts of their making
-|Understand continuity and change in genres, styles and traditions
-|Use a working vocabulary and specialist terminology
T|Skills
S|Recording, researching and evaluating
-|Record experiences and observations in drawing or other visual forms, research, and gather, select and organise visual information
-|Explore resources; analyse, discuss and evaluate images, objects and artefacts; make independent judgements
-|Use knowledge of others' work to develop and extend your own thinking and work
S|Developing, making and presenting
-|Generate and explore lines of enquiry with suitable media and techniques
-|Apply knowledge in making images and artefacts, review and modify work, and develop ideas using your own and others' evaluations
-|Organise, select and communicate ideas and solutions and present them in visual, tactile or sensory forms
-|Work entirely in digital media, entirely non-digital, or a mix, as long as the aims and assessment objectives are met
U|3.3 Art, craft and design
T|Experiences and drawing
S|Media and drawing
3.3|Experience a variety of 2D and/or 3D media, processes and techniques, traditional and new
-|Explore drawing for different purposes using varied methods, media and scales, optionally supported by sketchbooks, workbooks or journals
S|Studying other work
-|Explore images, artefacts and resources from past and recent art, craft and design, European and non-European, within investigating and making
-|Show responses to examples through practical and critical activities demonstrating understanding of styles, genres and traditions
-|Be aware of the four assessment objectives and the importance of process as well as product
T|Areas of study
S|Areas of study across titles
-|Show critical and contextual work through 2D and/or 3D processes and media in each component
-|In Component 1 show work from areas of study in two or more endorsed titles
-|In Component 2 show work from areas of study in one or more titles, same as or different from Component 1
T|Skills and techniques
S|Skills and techniques in chosen areas
-|Demonstrate the overarching skills in the context of the chosen areas of study across the titles
U|3.4 Fine art
T|Experiences and drawing
S|Fine art media and study of other work
3.4|Experience a range of fine art media, processes and techniques, traditional and new
-|Explore drawing for different purposes using varied methods, media and scales, optionally using sketchbooks
-|Explore images, artefacts and resources from past and recent art, craft and design, including non-European, integral to investigating and making
-|Show responses through practical and critical activities, and understand assessment objectives and the importance of process
T|Areas of study
S|Fine art areas
-|Work in one or more fine art areas, which may overlap or combine: drawing and painting, mixed media including collage and assemblage, sculpture, ceramics, installation
-|Work in printmaking (relief, intaglio, screen, lithography) and in moving image and photography
T|Skills and techniques
S|Recording, audience and imagery
-|Appreciate different approaches to recording images: observation, analysis, expression and imagination
-|Be aware of the intended audience or purpose of your fine art
-|Understand conventions of figurative and abstract imagery or genres
S|Ways of working and visual elements
-|Appreciate ways of working such as underpainting, glazing, wash and impasto; modelling, carving, casting, constructing and welding; etching, engraving, drypoint, mono, lino and screen printing and lithography
-|Understand pictorial space, composition, rhythm, scale and structure
-|Appreciate colour, line, tone, texture, shape and form
T|Knowledge and understanding
S|Fine art knowledge
-|Understand how ideas, feelings and meanings are conveyed and interpreted in fine art
-|Know historical and contemporary developments, styles and genres
-|Understand how work relates to social, environmental, cultural and ethical contexts and to time and place
-|Understand continuity and change in fine art styles, genres and traditions
-|Use vocabulary and specialist terms relevant to fine art
