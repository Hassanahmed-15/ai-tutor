U|Unit 4: Inference for Quantitative Data: Means
T|4.1 Sampling Distributions for Sample Means
S|Sampling distribution of a sample mean
4.1.A|Calculate the mean and standard deviation of the sampling distribution of a sample mean
4.1.A.1|The sample mean has mean mu and standard deviation sigma over root n
4.1.B|Justify the conditions for the sampling distribution of a sample mean
4.1.B.1|Check randomization and the 10 percent condition when sampling without replacement
4.1.B.2|With a normal population, the sample mean is normal at any sample size
4.1.B.3|Otherwise it is approximately normal when n is at least 30, and more for extreme skew
4.1.C|Interpret the mean, standard deviation and probabilities of this sampling distribution
4.1.C.1|Interpret in the context of the population
T|4.2 Constructing a Confidence Interval for a Population Mean or Population Mean Difference
S|t-distributions and choosing a procedure
4.2.A|Describe t-distributions
4.2.A.1|t-distributions are symmetric, bell-shaped with heavier tails, set by degrees of freedom, approaching the standard normal as df grows
4.2.A.2|Use t when sigma is unknown and s must be used
4.2.B|Identify the interval procedure and parameter for a mean or mean difference
4.2.B.1|Use a one-sample t-interval for a population mean
4.2.B.2|For matched pairs, take differences and use a one-sample t-interval for the mean difference
4.2.B.3|State the parameter in context, including the order of subtraction for differences
S|Conditions for the t-interval
4.2.C|Verify conditions for a one-sample t-interval
4.2.C.1|Check randomization, the 10 percent condition, and the sample data condition (normal population, n at least 30, or no strong skew or outliers)
S|Computing the interval
4.2.D|Calculate a confidence interval for a mean or mean difference
4.2.D.1|The point estimate is the sample mean or mean difference
4.2.D.2|The interval is x-bar plus or minus t-star times s over root n, with n-1 degrees of freedom
4.2.E|Calculate standard error and margin of error for a one-sample t-interval
4.2.E.1|Standard error of the mean is s over root n
4.2.E.2|Margin of error is t-star times the standard error
T|4.3 Justifying a Claim Based on a Confidence Interval for a Population Mean or Population Mean Difference
S|Interpreting a confidence interval
4.3.A|Interpret a confidence interval for a mean or mean difference in context
4.3.A.1|The computed interval may or may not contain the true value
4.3.A.2|Confidence level is the long-run percentage of intervals that capture the parameter
4.3.A.3|State we are C percent confident the interval contains the parameter, in context
S|Claims and interval width
4.3.B|Justify a claim from the interval
4.3.B.1|The interval gives plausible values that can support a claim
4.3.C|Relate sample size, width, confidence level and margin of error
4.3.C.1|Higher confidence raises the critical value, margin of error and width
4.3.C.2|Larger samples shrink standard error and narrow the interval, about one over root n

S|Procedure and hypotheses
4.4.A|Identify the testing method and parameter for a mean or mean difference with unknown sigma
4.4.A.1|Use a one-sample t-test for a population mean
4.4.A.2|For matched pairs, take differences and use a one-sample t-test for the mean difference
4.4.A.3|State the parameter in context
4.4.B|Identify null and alternative hypotheses
4.4.B.1|H0: mu equals mu0, with one-sided or two-sided Ha
4.4.B.2|For differences, H0: mu-d equals 0, with one-sided or two-sided Ha
S|Conditions for the t-test
4.4.C|Verify conditions for a one-sample t-test
4.4.C.1|Check randomization, the 10 percent condition and the sample data condition
T|4.5 Carrying Out a Test for a Population Mean or Population Mean Difference
S|Test statistic and p-value
4.5.A|Calculate the test statistic and p-value for a mean or mean difference
4.5.A.1|t equals (x-bar minus mu0) over (s over root n) with n-1 degrees of freedom
4.5.A.2|Find the p-value from the t-distribution using a table or technology
4.5.B|Interpret the p-value
4.5.B.1|The p-value assumes the null hypothesis is true
S|Decisions and conclusions
4.5.C|Justify a claim from the test
4.5.C.1|Reject if the p-value is at most alpha; otherwise fail to reject
4.5.C.2|Results support the answer to the investigative question
4.5.C.3|State the conclusion in context, tied to Ha, with non-definitive language
T|4.6 Sampling Distributions for the Difference Between Two Sample Means
S|Difference of two sample means
4.6.A|Calculate the mean and standard deviation of the sampling distribution of a difference of sample means
4.6.A.1|The difference has mean mu1 minus mu2 and standard deviation root of sigma1 squared over n1 plus sigma2 squared over n2
4.6.B|Justify conditions for this sampling distribution
4.6.B.1|Check two independent random samples and the 10 percent condition for each
4.6.B.2|For experiments only random assignment is needed
4.6.B.3|It is normal if both populations are normal
4.6.B.4|Otherwise it is approximately normal if both n are at least 30
4.6.C|Interpret the mean, standard deviation and probabilities
4.6.C.1|Interpret within the context of the specific populations
T|4.7 Constructing a Confidence Interval for the Difference Between Two Population Means
S|Procedure and conditions
4.7.A|Identify the interval procedure and parameter for a difference of means
4.7.A.1|Use a two-sample t-interval for independent samples
4.7.A.2|State the parameter as the difference in means, response variable and populations
4.7.B|Verify conditions for a two-sample t-interval
4.7.B.1|Check randomization, the 10 percent condition (not needed in experiments) and the sample data condition for both samples
S|Computing the interval
4.7.C|Calculate the interval for a difference of means
4.7.C.1|The point estimate is x1-bar minus x2-bar
4.7.C.2|The interval is the point estimate plus or minus t-star times the standard error, with df found by technology
4.7.D|Calculate standard error and margin of error for a difference of means
4.7.D.1|Standard error is root of s1 squared over n1 plus s2 squared over n2
4.7.D.2|Margin of error is t-star times standard error

4.8.B|Justify a claim from the interval
