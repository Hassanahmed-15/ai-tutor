U|Unit 3: Work, Energy, and Power
T|3.1 Translational Kinetic Energy
S|Translational kinetic energy
3.1.A|Describe the translational kinetic energy of an object from its mass and velocity
3.1.A.1|Kinetic energy equals one half mass times speed squared
3.1.A.2|Kinetic energy is a scalar
3.1.A.3|Observers in different reference frames measure different kinetic energies
T|3.2 Work
S|Work and conservative forces
3.2.A|Describe the work done on an object or system by a force or collection of forces
3.2.A.1|Work is energy transferred into or out of a system by a force acting over a distance; conservative work is path independent, nonconservative work is not
3.2.A.2|Work is a scalar that can be positive, negative or zero
3.2.A.3|Work by a constant force depends on the force component parallel to the displacement
S|Work-energy theorem
3.2.A.4|Net work equals the change in kinetic energy (work-energy theorem), and friction dissipates energy equal to friction force times path length
3.2.A.5|Work equals the area under a force versus displacement graph
T|3.3 Potential Energy
S|Potential energy of a system
3.3.A|Describe the potential energy of a system
3.3.A.1|A system has potential energy when its objects interact only through conservative forces
3.3.A.2|Potential energy is a scalar tied to the positions of objects in the system
3.3.A.3|The zero of potential energy is chosen by the observer for convenience
3.3.A.4|Use elastic, general gravitational and near-surface gravitational potential energy formulas
3.3.A.5|Total potential energy of many objects is the sum over pairs
T|3.4 Conservation of Energy
S|Energies present in a system
3.4.A|Describe the energies present in a system
3.4.A.1|A single-object system can have only kinetic energy
3.4.A.2|Systems with conservative interactions or reversible shape changes can have kinetic and potential energy
S|Conservation of mechanical energy
3.4.B|Describe a system's behavior using conservation of mechanical energy
3.4.B.1|Mechanical energy is kinetic plus potential energy
3.4.B.2|A change in one energy type is balanced by changes in others or by transfer with the surroundings
3.4.B.3|A system can be chosen so its total energy is constant
3.4.B.4|A change in total energy equals the energy transferred in or out
S|System choice and energy
3.4.C|Describe how choosing the system determines whether its energy changes
3.4.C.1|Energy is conserved in all interactions
3.4.C.2|With zero work and no nonconservative interactions, mechanical energy is constant
3.4.C.3|Nonzero work means energy moves between system and environment
T|3.5 Power
S|Power
3.5.A|Describe energy transfer into, out of or within a system in terms of power
3.5.A.1|Power is the rate of energy change over time
3.5.A.2|Average power is energy transferred divided by time
3.5.A.3|Average power equals total work divided by time
3.5.A.4|Instantaneous power from a constant force equals force component along velocity times speed
U|Unit 4: Linear Momentum
T|4.1 Linear Momentum
S|Linear momentum
4.1.A|Describe the linear momentum of an object or system
4.1.A.1|Momentum equals mass times velocity
4.1.A.2|Momentum is a vector in the direction of velocity
4.1.A.3|Use momentum to analyse collisions and explosions, using the object model for the initial and final states
T|4.2 Change in Momentum and Impulse
S|Impulse
4.2.A|Describe the impulse delivered to an object or system
4.2.A.1|Net external force is the rate of change of momentum
4.2.A.2|Impulse is average force times the time it acts
4.2.A.3|Impulse is a vector along the net force
4.2.A.4|Impulse equals the area under a force versus time graph
4.2.A.5|Net force equals the slope of a momentum versus time graph
S|Impulse-momentum theorem
4.2.B|Relate the impulse on a system to its change in momentum
4.2.B.1|Change in momentum is final minus initial momentum
4.2.B.2|Impulse equals change in momentum (impulse-momentum theorem)
4.2.B.3|Newton's second law follows from the impulse-momentum theorem for constant mass
T|4.3 Conservation of Linear Momentum
S|Conserving momentum in a system
4.3.A|Describe a system's behavior using conservation of linear momentum
4.3.A.1|A collection of objects has one center-of-mass velocity, constant with no net external force
4.3.A.2|Total momentum is the sum of the parts' momenta
4.3.A.3|Without external forces, momentum changes inside a system cancel; changes in system momentum equal the impulse from outside
4.3.A.4|Apply conservation of momentum to find velocities just before and after collisions or explosions
S|System choice and momentum
4.3.B|Describe how choosing the system determines whether its momentum changes
4.3.B.1|Momentum is conserved in all interactions
4.3.B.2|With zero net external force, total momentum is constant
4.3.B.3|With nonzero net external force, momentum is exchanged with the environment
T|4.4 Elastic and Inelastic Collisions
S|Elastic and inelastic collisions
4.4.A|Describe whether an interaction between objects is elastic or inelastic
4.4.A.1|Elastic collisions keep total kinetic energy the same
4.4.A.2|In an elastic collision individual kinetic energies may still change
4.4.A.3|Inelastic collisions lose total kinetic energy
4.4.A.4|Lost kinetic energy is converted by nonconservative forces into other forms
4.4.A.5|In a perfectly inelastic collision the objects stick together
