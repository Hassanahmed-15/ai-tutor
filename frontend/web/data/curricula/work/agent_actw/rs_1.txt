U|Big Idea 1: Question and Explore
T|EU 1.1 Curiosity drives investigation
S|Contextualizing a problem
LO 1.1A|Place a problem or issue in context and identify its complexities
EK 1.1A1|Others' perspectives prompt new questions; inquiry starts by narrowing interest, finding the problem and its origins, and situating it in a larger context
S|Posing questions with multiple perspectives
LO 1.1B|Ask questions and seek answers reflecting multiple, divergent or contradictory perspectives
EK 1.1B1|Good research questions account for the complexity of the issue
EK 1.1B2|Inquiry uses curiosity and imagination to explore approaches to complex issues
S|Choosing a topic of inquiry
LO 1.1C|Identify a topic of inquiry
EK 1.1C1|Topics come from personal interest, passion for a field, wanting to understand more, or addressing a real-world issue
EK 1.1C2|Explore the knowledge base and perspectives, then adjust scope to the project's limits and resources
EK 1.1C3|Inquiry reveals connections that raise curiosity and further questions
S|Purpose and significance of inquiry
LO 1.1D|Explain the purpose and significance of scholarly inquiry
EK 1.1D1|Scholars explore, explain and create
EK 1.1D2|Inquiry addresses practical, theoretical, interpretive or aesthetic problems, or confirms, challenges or extends ideas
EK 1.1D3|Situate inquiry within the scholarly community it matters to
S|Writing a research question
LO 1.1E|Develop and revise a focused research question or project goal
EK 1.1E1|The question or goal grows from the scholar's purpose
EK 1.1E2|Questions usually need several revisions for scope and feasibility
T|EU 1.2 Building on what is known
S|Using prior knowledge
LO 1.2A|Retrieve, question, organise and use prior knowledge about a topic
EK 1.2A1|Understanding comes from experience, sources, culture and assumptions, not just collecting information
EK 1.2A2|Brainstorming, concept maps, prewriting and drafting help organise and connect ideas
EK 1.2A3|Inquiry confirms or challenges existing understanding and assumptions
T|EU 1.3 Managing resources and information
S|Accessing and managing information
LO 1.3A|Access and manage information with effective strategies
EK 1.3A1|Information comes from secondary sources and from primary sources such as original works or collected data
EK 1.3A2|Online databases and libraries hold secondary and some primary sources
EK 1.3A3|Advanced search tools, Boolean logic and keywords refine searches
EK 1.3A4|Source bibliographies can lead to more resources
EK 1.3A5|Social media can be a source if its limitations are understood
EK 1.3A6|Citation and reference software helps manage sources and bibliographies
EK 1.3A7|Survey and statistics tools help collect and analyse large data sets
T|EU 1.4 Relevance and credibility of sources
S|Evaluating sources
LO 1.4A|Evaluate the relevance and credibility of sources and data for the inquiry
EK 1.4A1|Research scope, purpose and source credibility affect the generalisability and reliability of conclusions
EK 1.4A2|Credible evidence comes from relevant, reliable, current and authoritative sources
EK 1.4A3|Judge credibility by the author's reputation, perspective and methods, and peer review
EK 1.4A4|Survey data accuracy depends on respondent honesty and memory and on instrument design
T|EU 1.5 Methods aligned with purpose
S|Identifying needed information
LO 1.5A|Identify the information needed for the inquiry's context
EK 1.5A1|How a problem is framed guides the information and methods needed
S|Designing and carrying out inquiry
LO 1.5B|Design, plan and carry out a scholarly inquiry
EK 1.5B1|Align methods of collection, analysis, innovation or interpretation with the question or goal
EK 1.5B2|Methods may be qualitative, quantitative, mixed, or artistic processes
EK 1.5B3|Adjust the course, tools and methods as scope and feasibility become clear
EK 1.5B4|Artistic processes combine research methods with shaping media and form
EK 1.5B5|Choose qualitative, quantitative or mixed data collection to fit the question
S|Analysing and combining data
EK 1.5B6|Analyse data in ways suited to the inquiry
EK 1.5B7|Justify the sample chosen and know the limits of conclusions drawn from it
EK 1.5B8|Use descriptive or inferential statistics to display and analyse data
EK 1.5B9|Code and categorise data to find patterns or themes
EK 1.5B10|Combine qualitative and quantitative data to triangulate trends and patterns
S|Perseverance on long projects
LO 1.5C|Show perseverance by setting goals, managing time and working independently
EK 1.5C1|Plan methods and activities around deadlines, priorities, risks and others' availability
EK 1.5C2|Expect setbacks, keep to essential goals and try alternatives
EK 1.5C3|Seek expert guidance while staying self-sufficient
S|Ethical research
LO 1.5D|Use ethical research practices
EK 1.5D1|Researchers have ethical and moral responsibilities
EK 1.5D2|Laws and rules govern research on humans and animals, including institutional review board approval
EK 1.5D3|Copyright and patent rules govern use of others' work and information
U|Big Idea 2: Understand and Analyze
T|EU 2.1 Comprehending an author's perspective
S|Reading critically
LO 2.1A|Use suitable reading strategies and read critically for a purpose
EK 2.1A1|Read closely for main idea, tone, assumptions, context, perspective, reasoning and evidence
EK 2.1A2|Preview and prioritise text by skimming, scanning, rereading and questioning
EK 2.1A3|Make meaning by annotating, note-taking, highlighting and reading aloud
EK 2.1A4|Perspectives are shared through written, spoken, visual or performance texts and include attitude or tone
S|Summarizing a text's main idea
LO 2.1B|Summarise and explain a text's main idea or aim without oversimplifying
EK 2.1B1|The main idea sits in the thesis, claim or conclusion, or is implied
EK 2.1B2|Artistic works convey a perspective; analyse context, subject, structure, style and aesthetic
T|EU 2.2 Evidence and reasoning in arguments
S|Line of reasoning
LO 2.2A|Explain and analyse the logic and line of reasoning of an argument
EK 2.2A1|A line of reasoning is claims justified by evidence
EK 2.2A2|Reasoning is organised by purpose, such as causality, definition or solution
EK 2.2A3|Distinguish inductive reasoning from deductive reasoning
EK 2.2A4|Missing the complexity of an argument leads to oversimplification
EK 2.2A5|Strong arguments acknowledge and answer other arguments with concession, refutation or rebuttal
S|Evaluating evidence
LO 2.2B|Evaluate the relevance and credibility of evidence in context
EK 2.2B1|Context and situation relative to other arguments shape interpretation
EK 2.2B2|Qualitative and quantitative evidence vary in validity
EK 2.2B3|Authors choose evidence strategically
EK 2.2B4|Writers may appeal to or manipulate readers through language, authority, qualifiers, fallacies and emphasis
EK 2.2B5|Evidence can show comparative, causal or correlational relationships and trends
EK 2.2B6|Credibility drops when authors ignore limitations, opposing views or their own biases
S|Validity of an argument
LO 2.2C|Evaluate the validity of an argument
EK 2.2C1|An argument is valid when reasoning and conclusion align logically
EK 2.2C2|Validity needs evidence aligned with conclusions and acknowledged limits
EK 2.2C3|Conclusions are contextual and must be affirmed, qualified or refuted
S|Critiquing others' work
LO 2.2D|Evaluate and critique others' inquiries, studies, works and perspectives
EK 2.2D1|Judge studies on internal coherence and alignment of purpose, goals and methods
T|EU 2.3 Implications of arguments
S|Implications of a claim
LO 2.3A|Connect an argument to broader issues through the implications of its claim
EK 2.3A1|Arguments' consequences may be intended or unintended
S|Evaluating resolutions
LO 2.3B|Evaluate potential resolutions or solutions raised by an argument
EK 2.3B1|Arguments can change behaviour and have real-world impact
U|Big Idea 3: Evaluate Multiple Perspectives
T|EU 3.1 Competing perspectives
S|Comparing perspectives
LO 3.1A|Identify, compare and interpret multiple perspectives or arguments on an issue
EK 3.1A1|Perspective is shaped by background, assumptions, worldview and outside sources
EK 3.1A2|Perspectives may concur, complement or compete
EK 3.1A3|Some perspectives are ambiguous, so interpretation may not give a definite answer
T|EU 3.2 Not all arguments are equal
S|Evaluating competing arguments
LO 3.2A|Evaluate alternate, opposing or competing arguments by their implications and limitations
EK 3.2A1|Arguments may appeal to emotion, values, bias, assumptions and logic
EK 3.2A2|Consider how your own biases and assumptions influence judgment
