U|Big Idea 4: Synthesize Ideas
T|EU 4.1 Scholarly works convey perspectives
S|Building a well-reasoned argument
LO 4.1A|Build a well-reasoned argument that accounts for the problem's complexity
EK 4.1A1|Effective arguments use reason and evidence to convey a perspective stated in the thesis or conclusion
EK 4.1A2|Carefully chosen, connected claims, reasons and evidence unify an argument
EK 4.1A3|Qualifiers limit a claim and avoid overgeneralising
EK 4.1A4|Answer other arguments with concession, refutation or rebuttal
EK 4.1A5|A line of reasoning is a clear logical path to a conclusion
EK 4.1A6|Reasoning can be deductive or inductive
S|Organizing reasoning and process
EK 4.1A7|Organise the line of reasoning by the argument's purpose
EK 4.1A8|Arrange claims and evidence spatially, chronologically or by importance to show relationships
EK 4.1A9|The same argument can be organised differently for different audiences
EK 4.1A10|Choose a process aligned with the inquiry or project goal
EK 4.1A11|Articulate your choices, even where they leave ambiguity
EK 4.1A12|An aesthetic rationale is a reasoned argument for formal and stylistic choices in an artwork
S|Disciplinary approaches
LO 4.1B|Select and apply a disciplinary or interdisciplinary approach to a scholarly argument or aesthetic rationale
EK 4.1B1|Each discipline has its own conventions for knowing, questioning and communicating
EK 4.1B2|Use discipline-specific terminology in analysis
EK 4.1B3|Foundational texts reveal a discipline's ways of knowing and valuing information
EK 4.1B4|Disciplines can be broad, narrow, intersecting or combined
T|EU 4.2 Engaging responsibly with evidence
S|Synthesizing evidence
LO 4.2A|Interpret, use and synthesise qualitative and quantitative information from varied sources to support an argument
EK 4.2A1|Evidence comes from print and nonprint sources, experts and field data
EK 4.2A2|Compelling evidence is sufficient, accurate, relevant, current and credible
EK 4.2A3|Choose evidence by context, purpose and audience
EK 4.2A4|Selected and attributed evidence builds your argument's credibility
S|Linking evidence with claims
LO 4.2B|Write insightful commentary that links evidence with claims
EK 4.2B1|Commentary connects evidence to the claim by interpretation or inference, patterns, trends and relationships
T|EU 4.3 Acknowledging others' contributions
S|Attribution and citation
LO 4.3A|Attribute knowledge and ideas accurately and ethically with a citation style
EK 4.3A1|Accurate attribution enhances credibility
EK 4.3A2|Avoid plagiarism by acknowledging sources thoroughly
EK 4.3A3|Introduce, integrate or embed source material in the argument
EK 4.3A4|Cite quoted and paraphrased material following a style manual
EK 4.3A5|Disciplines use specific style guides such as APA, MLA or Chicago
EK 4.3A6|Appropriation in art has legal and ethical implications
T|EU 4.4 Forming one's own perspective
S|Innovating and extending ideas
LO 4.4A|Extend an idea, question, process or product to create new understanding
EK 4.4A1|Innovative solutions challenge assumptions, imagine alternatives and use reflective skepticism
T|EU 4.5 Consequences of arguments and choices
S|Resolutions and conclusions
LO 4.5A|Offer resolutions or solutions based on evidence, limitations and implications
EK 4.5A1|Weigh advantages and disadvantages of options against the goal in context
U|Big Idea 5: Team, Transform, and Transmit
T|EU 5.1 How presentation affects interpretation
S|Producing a cohesive academic paper
LO 5.1A|Plan and produce a cohesive academic paper for an audience, context and purpose
EK 5.1A1|Present conclusions with introduction and gap, method, results, discussion, conclusion and bibliography
EK 5.1A2|Achieve coherence with logical flow and transitions
S|Grammar, usage, style and mechanics
LO 5.1B|Follow conventions of grammar, usage, style and mechanics
EK 5.1B1|Express tone through word choice, sentence structure and imagery
EK 5.1B2|Vary sentences for emphasis with agreement, modifier placement and tense consistency
EK 5.1B3|Precise word choice cuts confusion and wordiness
EK 5.1B4|Spelling and grammar errors weaken credibility
S|Media and design
LO 5.1C|Communicate through suitable media using effective design
EK 5.1C1|Headings, layout, illustrations and captions aid engagement when used well
EK 5.1C2|Present data graphically to aid understanding
EK 5.1C3|Choose the medium by context, purpose and audience
S|Adapting for audience
LO 5.1D|Adapt an argument to context, purpose and audience
EK 5.1D1|Adapt by selecting and emphasising information for audience, situation, medium and purpose
EK 5.1D2|Explain choices in non-specialist language for outsiders
S|Delivery and performance
LO 5.1E|Engage an audience with effective delivery or performance
EK 5.1E1|Vary volume, tempo, movement, eye contact and energy to emphasise and engage
EK 5.1E2|Present work in discipline-specific or cross-disciplinary forms
EK 5.1E3|Present after revision and rehearsal, using feedback and self-critique
S|Defending inquiry choices
LO 5.1F|Defend inquiry choices and the final product with clarity and conviction
EK 5.1F1|Explain the rationale for choices in relation to the finished work
EK 5.1F2|Respond thoughtfully to audience critiques and questions
T|EU 5.2 Effective teams
S|Contributing to a team
LO 5.2A|Contribute individually to a collaborative effort
EK 5.2A1|Sharing your strengths and challenges makes contributions more effective
S|Team climate and conflict
LO 5.2B|Build a constructive team climate, resolve conflicts and include all members
EK 5.2B1|Teams are built around tasks; low-risk teambuilding helps
EK 5.2B2|Teams work best when they understand their diverse perspectives, talents and skills
EK 5.2B3|Use interpersonal communication, consensus building, conflict resolution and negotiation
EK 5.2B4|Consider online collaboration tools
T|EU 5.3 Reflection
S|Reflecting on your own work
LO 5.3A|Reflect on and revise your writing, thinking and creative processes
EK 5.3A1|Reflection is ongoing and may use journals, self-questioning or drawing
EK 5.3A2|Learning needs iterative rethinking, revising and rewriting
EK 5.3A3|Stay mindful of the rationale behind chosen methods
EK 5.3A4|Reflect on how inquiry deepened understanding and self-direction
S|Reflecting on collaboration and significance
LO 5.3B|Reflect on collaborative experiences
EK 5.3B1|Note how actions and assumptions helped or hindered group and individual tasks
LO 5.3C|Reflect on the larger significance of the inquiry process and finished work
EK 5.3C1|Explore future directions for inquiry and scholarship
EK 5.3C2|Inquiry can transform understanding and one's identity as a scholar
T|EU 5.4 Scholarly community and peer review
S|Peer review
LO 5.4A|Give constructive peer review suited to a project's stage
EK 5.4A1|Peer review follows guidelines and criteria suited to the work
LO 5.4B|Receive and consider peer review of your work
EK 5.4B1|Peer review sharpens your critical eye and your work
EK 5.4B2|Scholarly communities do best when members seek and give feedback
