#Unit 3: Work, Energy, and Power
=3.1 Translational Kinetic Energy
3.1.A|Kinetic energy|Describe translational kinetic energy in terms of mass and velocity.
3.1.A.1|Kinetic energy|Compute translational kinetic energy as one half m v^2.
3.1.A.2|Kinetic energy|Note kinetic energy depends on the observer's reference frame.
3.1.A.3|Kinetic energy|Note kinetic energy is a scalar.
=3.2 Work
3.2.A|Work|Describe the work done on an object or system by a force or forces.
3.2.A.1|Work and conservative forces|Define work as energy transferred by a force over a distance; contrast conservative and nonconservative forces.
3.2.A.2|Work and conservative forces|Note work is a scalar that can be positive, negative or zero.
3.2.A.3|Computing work|Compute work of a variable force by integrating the dot product along the path.
3.2.A.4|Work-energy theorem|Apply the work-energy theorem: net work equals change in kinetic energy; include friction dissipation.
3.2.A.5|Work-energy theorem|Find work as the area under a parallel-force versus displacement graph.
=3.3 Potential Energy
3.3.A|Potential energy|Describe the potential energy of a system.
3.3.A.1|Potential energy|Recognize potential energy needs conservative interactions among objects in a system.
3.3.A.2|Potential energy|Note potential energy is a scalar tied to position within a system.
3.3.A.3|Potential energy|Choose the zero of potential energy for convenience.
3.3.A.4|Force and potential energy|Relate conservative force to potential energy.
3.3.A.5|Force and potential energy|Find the 1D conservative force as minus the slope of U(x), pointing toward lower U.
3.3.A.6|Equilibrium on U graphs|Use U(x) graphs; stable equilibrium at a minimum, unstable at a maximum.
3.3.A.7|Common potential energies|Use spring, gravitational (-Gm1m2/r) and near-surface potential energy forms.
3.3.A.8|Common potential energies|Sum pairwise potential energies for systems of more than two objects.
=3.4 Conservation of Energy
3.4.A|Energy in systems|Describe the energies present in a system.
3.4.A.1|Energy in systems|Note a single-object system can have only kinetic energy.
3.4.A.2|Energy in systems|Note systems with conservative interactions or reversible shape change can have both kinetic and potential energy.
3.4.B|Conservation of mechanical energy|Describe system behavior using conservation of mechanical energy.
3.4.B.1|Conservation of mechanical energy|Define mechanical energy as kinetic plus potential.
3.4.B.2|Conservation of mechanical energy|Balance energy changes within a system or by transfer with surroundings.
3.4.B.3|Conservation of mechanical energy|Choose a system whose total energy is constant.
3.4.B.4|Conservation of mechanical energy|Equate a system's energy change to energy transferred in or out.
3.4.C|System choice|Describe how choosing the system decides whether its energy changes.
3.4.C.1|System choice|State energy is conserved in all interactions.
3.4.C.2|System choice|Conclude mechanical energy is constant if net work is zero and no nonconservative interactions exist.
3.4.C.3|System choice|Conclude nonzero work transfers energy between system and environment.
=3.5 Power
3.5.A|Power|Describe energy transfer into, out of or within a system in terms of power.
3.5.A.1|Power|Define power as the rate of energy change over time.
3.5.A.2|Power|Define average power as energy transferred over time.
3.5.A.3|Power|Compute average power as work divided by time.
3.5.A.4|Power|Compute instantaneous power as dW/dt.
3.5.A.5|Power|Compute instantaneous power as force parallel to velocity times speed.
#Unit 4: Linear Momentum
=4.1 Linear Momentum
4.1.A|Linear momentum|Describe the linear momentum of an object or system.
4.1.A.1|Linear momentum|Define momentum as mass times velocity.
4.1.A.2|Linear momentum|Note momentum is a vector in the direction of velocity.
4.1.A.3|Linear momentum|Use momentum to analyze collisions and explosions.
=4.2 Change in Momentum and Impulse
4.2.A|Impulse|Describe the impulse delivered to an object or system.
4.2.A.1|Impulse|State net external force equals the rate of change of momentum.
4.2.A.2|Impulse|Define impulse as the integral of force over time.
4.2.A.3|Impulse|Note impulse is a vector along the net force.
4.2.A.4|Impulse|Find impulse as the area under a net force versus time graph.
4.2.A.5|Impulse|Find net force as the slope of a momentum versus time graph.
4.2.B|Impulse-momentum theorem|Relate impulse to change in momentum.
4.2.B.1|Impulse-momentum theorem|Define change in momentum as final minus initial.
4.2.B.2|Impulse-momentum theorem|Apply the impulse-momentum theorem, including constant-velocity changing-mass systems.
=4.3 Conservation of Linear Momentum
4.3.A|Conserving momentum|Describe system behavior using conservation of linear momentum.
4.3.A.1|Conserving momentum|Describe a collection as one system with a center-of-mass velocity that is constant without net external force.
4.3.A.2|Conserving momentum|Sum constituent momenta to get total momentum.
4.3.A.3|Conserving momentum|Balance momentum changes inside a system, or by impulse from surroundings.
4.3.A.4|Conserving momentum|Find velocities just before and after collisions or explosions.
4.3.B|System choice|Describe how choosing a system determines whether its momentum changes.
4.3.B.1|System choice|State momentum is conserved in all interactions.
4.3.B.2|System choice|Conclude momentum is constant if net external force is zero.
4.3.B.3|System choice|Conclude nonzero net external force transfers momentum with the environment.
=4.4 Elastic and Inelastic Collisions
4.4.A|Collision types|Describe whether an interaction is elastic or inelastic.
4.4.A.1|Collision types|Define an elastic collision as conserving total kinetic energy.
4.4.A.2|Collision types|Note individual kinetic energies may change in an elastic collision.
4.4.A.3|Collision types|Define an inelastic collision as losing total kinetic energy.
4.4.A.4|Collision types|Explain lost kinetic energy is transformed by nonconservative forces.
4.4.A.5|Collision types|Define a perfectly inelastic collision as objects sticking together.
